Between Employment and Business

A book about how to transition to your own venture without destroying life along the way.

Most entrepreneurship books begin too late — when the person has already quit, left their job, and is building a company. This book begins earlier: at the moment when the current job no longer works, but a business has not yet become reality.

Without cult of risk. Without promises of easy freedom. Without demanding you first believe in yourself.

The book helps you test an entrepreneurial hypothesis, build financial and psychological ground, gather first data, and make a decision: build a business, stay in employment, or pick your own combination of these models.

Who this book is for

This book is for people who:

This is not a book about how to make everyone become an entrepreneur. This is a book about how to stop guessing and run an honest experiment.

The main idea

The popular script looks like this: first quit, then full freedom, then comes the idea, motivation and a successful business.

In reality, quitting often destroys exactly the resources you need for a good decision: money, time for experiments, calm and the right to make mistakes.

A mature transition works differently:

Stay employed · Test a hypothesis · Keep the income · Build the foundation

The book shows how to walk this path without heroism or self-deception.

What will change after the book

The reader will be able to:

Listened to your own reasons for wanting to quit · Built financial cushion for a year of experiments · Created a working prototype with paying customers · Chosen the next step consciously: stay, leave, or move into a hybrid model

What's inside

Part I. Strip away the romance. Why employment is not a defeat, business is not automatic freedom, and entrepreneurship is needed by far from everyone.

Part II. Build a foundation. Money, skills, reputation, energy and space for experiments. Why confidence is not a necessary condition for action.

Part III. Learn to think like an experimenter. How to test hypotheses, interpret failures, notice mental traps and avoid confusing one failed test with a final verdict.

Part IV. Make a decision. When to leave, when to stay, how to spend the first 90 days and why returning to employment can be part of an entrepreneurial strategy.

Open book development

Working on the book is organized like product development. Chapters are published in small parts, ideas are discussed with readers, and practical tools are tested on real transitions between employment and business.

Feedback is used to:

Catch weak spots in the argument · Test tools on real transitions · Refine cases and examples · Improve clarity for readers who are making the decision right now

Not every comment will make it into the text. But every recurring problem becomes data.

Become an early reader

Early group participants receive:

Chapters as they are written · A channel for direct communication with the author · The ability to influence the final structure · Mention in the acknowledgments (if you wish)

Participants are asked not for literary editing, but for an honest answer:

What changed in your understanding? What stayed unclear? What action did you take after reading?

Participation is free. Materials arrive gradually. You can unsubscribe at any time.
